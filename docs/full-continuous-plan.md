# Full Continuous 100 km Plan

Target event: Thames Path Ultra Challenge, Full Continuous 100 km.

Event date: Saturday 12 September to Sunday 13 September 2026.

Route: Bishops Park / Putney to Henley-on-Thames.

## Planning Assumptions

- The event is one continuous effort, not the 2-day daylight option.
- Expect to be moving through Saturday and potentially overnight into Sunday.
- Official full-distance figure: 100 km and about 350 m climb.
- Official start window: Trailblazerz at 06:45, runners from about 07:00, other runners/joggers/walkers from about 07:00 to 09:00 or 09:30 depending on final instructions, Ultra March around 08:30.
- Official finish estimates: runners 9-16 hours, joggers 16-20 hours, walkers 20-36 hours.

## Operating Priorities

1. Keep effort easy early.
2. Prevent foot damage before it compounds.
3. Eat and drink on a schedule.
4. Keep checkpoints efficient.
5. Treat the night section as a planned phase, not an emergency.

## Checkpoint Routine

Use the same routine every time:

1. Refill bottles.
2. Eat something before leaving.
3. Check feet, socks, laces, and hotspots.
4. Add or remove layers.
5. Confirm next section distance.
6. Leave before comfort turns into inertia.

For minor stops, aim to be deliberate and short. For major stops at 50 km and 78 km, allow more time, but avoid fully cooling down unless there is a real issue.

## Midpoint Plan: Runnymede 50 km

Runnymede is the key reset.

- Eat the official hot meal if it works with the nutrition plan.
- Change socks.
- Consider changing shoes if the first pair is wet, rubbing, or swollen-feet fit has changed.
- Reapply blister prevention.
- Put on or prepare night layers depending on expected pace.
- Check head torch, backup light, phone, watch, and battery.
- Leave with a clear plan for the next 13 km to Eton Wick.

## Night Section Plan

The full continuous format can easily run into darkness.

- Carry a tested head torch.
- Carry a backup light.
- Carry enough battery for phone/watch navigation and communication.
- Have a warmer layer accessible before sunset.
- Use reflective or high-visibility detail where practical.
- Keep caffeine as a planned tool, not an early crutch.
- Expect pace to drop after dark and after 70 km.

## Nutrition Plan

This needs testing in training, but the working target is:

- Eat every 30-45 minutes.
- Prefer familiar, repeatable foods.
- Use official checkpoint food as support, not the whole plan.
- Carry emergency calories that still seem edible when tired.
- Practise the 50-80 km food pattern during peak training weekends.

## Gear Decisions To Make

- Primary shoes.
- Backup shoes or larger second-half shoes.
- Sock system and number of spare pairs.
- Blister prevention approach.
- Pack or vest.
- Waterproof jacket.
- Warm layer for night.
- Head torch and backup light.
- Battery pack and cables.
- Bottle/flask setup.
- Poles, only if trained with and allowed by final event instructions.

## Finish and Recovery

- Plan transport from Henley before event day.
- Assume finish time may be awkward for normal transport.
- Pack dry clothes for immediately after finishing.
- Do not rely on making complex decisions at the finish.
- Have a simple food, fluids, warmth, and travel-home sequence ready.
