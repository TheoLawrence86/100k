# Full Continuous 100 km Training Plan

This is a working 14-week plan from Saturday 13 June 2026 to the Thames Path Ultra Challenge weekend on Saturday 12 September 2026. Week 1 is a two-day baseline weekend; full weekly rhythm starts Monday 15 June.

It is intentionally conservative until current fitness, injury history, and target pace are known.

The live day-by-day plan is in `data/training-plan.tsv`. The local viewer is `index.html`.

## Assumptions

- Target event: Thames Path Ultra Challenge 2026.
- Confirmed planning target: Full Continuous 100 km.
- Primary goal: finish comfortably and avoid avoidable foot, fuelling, pacing, and logistics failures.
- Terrain: flat Thames Path, likely repetitive underfoot, with low elevation but high cumulative foot stress.
- Main event-specific risks: going too hard early, foot damage, under-fuelling, night fatigue, and losing time at checkpoints.

## Weekly Structure

- 1 long easy session.
- 1 back-to-back tired-leg session most weeks after baseline is established.
- 2-3 short easy aerobic sessions.
- 1-2 strength and mobility sessions.
- 1 full rest day.
- Kit and nutrition testing during long sessions, not on event day.
- At least two sessions that finish in darkness or use the planned night kit.

## Coaching Rules

- Easy means easy enough to talk.
- Long sessions are for feet, food, kit, and patience.
- Eat every 30-45 minutes on long sessions.
- Practise 30-60 g carbs per hour in training.
- Peak weekend is 40 km + 22 km, not a solo 70 km damage session.
- Taper reduces volume, not all movement.

## Phase 1: Establish Baseline

Weeks 1-3: 13 June to 28 June 2026.

- Confirm current weekly mileage/time-on-feet.
- Build consistency without chasing big numbers.
- Start foot-care testing: shoes, socks, taping, blister prevention.
- Add simple strength: calves, glutes, hamstrings, quads, hips, trunk.
- Long session target: steady time-on-feet, not pace.
- Start noting what foods stay appealing after 2-4 hours.

## Phase 2: Build Specific Endurance

Weeks 4-8: 29 June to 2 August 2026.

- Increase long-session duration gradually.
- Add back-to-back sessions to simulate starting tired.
- Include flat, runnable/walkable river-path or canal-path terrain when possible.
- Practise eating every 30-45 minutes.
- Practise drinking and electrolytes in warm conditions.
- Test head torch, backup light, layers, and charging.
- Rehearse efficient stops: refill, eat, foot check, layer check, leave.

## Phase 3: Peak Specificity

Weeks 9-11: 3 August to 23 August 2026.

- Run or walk the most event-like long sessions.
- Do the biggest back-to-back weekend in this block.
- Finalise shoes, socks, pack, poles if used, rain kit, lights, and charging.
- Rehearse checkpoint behavior: arrive, refill, eat, check feet, leave.
- Include one long session or back-to-back sequence that deliberately starts tired and finishes after dark.
- Decide the Runnymede 50 km reset plan: food, socks, shoes, layers, battery, and blister care.
- By Saturday 22 August 2026, confirm optional extras, logistics, and fundraising deadlines.

## Phase 4: Taper and Execution

Weeks 12-14: 24 August to 12 September 2026.

- Reduce volume while keeping easy frequency.
- Avoid last-minute kit experiments.
- Keep short strides or light pace changes only if already normal in training.
- Prioritise sleep, foot health, and nutrition consistency.
- Pack early and check against the official kit list.
- Keep feet healthy. No aggressive new shoes, socks, insoles, or taping experiments.

## Event Execution Notes

- Start easier than feels necessary.
- Treat foot issues immediately.
- Keep eating before hunger and drinking before thirst.
- Use checkpoints efficiently; sitting too long can make restarting harder.
- Treat Runnymede at 50 km as a controlled reset, not a finish-line rehearsal.
- Expect the night section to be more mental and logistical than athletic.
- After 70 km, protect forward motion. Short, planned stops are better than long, vague stops.

## Metrics to Track

- Weekly total distance or time-on-feet.
- Longest single session.
- Biggest back-to-back weekend.
- Feet: blisters, hotspots, toenails, shoe pressure.
- Fuelling: calories per hour tolerated.
- Hydration: fluid and electrolyte approach.
- Sleep and fatigue.
- Any pain that changes gait.

## Research Basis

- Ultra Challenge's 100 km plan uses progressive weekly distance, core sessions, cutback weeks, and long weekend trail sessions.
- CALM's Ultra Challenge walking guide recommends gradual increases, event-kit training, weekly long walks, and 100 km milestones including 40 km with full kit and back-to-back 30 km walks.
- ISSN ultra-marathon nutrition guidance supports practising regular intake during long efforts, with race intake around 150-400 kcal/hour and 30-50 g carbohydrate/hour for many athletes.
- Taper research supports reducing training volume before endurance events while keeping some frequency and intensity.
